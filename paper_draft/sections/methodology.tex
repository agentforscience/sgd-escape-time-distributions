\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\para{Problem formulation.}
Let $\tstar$ be a strict local minimum of a regularized loss $L$, with Hessian eigenvalues up to $\lmax$.
We start $R$ independent replicas of an optimizer at $\tstar$, with separate minibatch draws.
The escape time $T$ of a replica is the first step at which gradient flow from $\vtheta_t$ no longer returns to $\tstar$.
We write $\tau$ for the Kaplan--Meier median of $T$.
We compare the Weibull survival function with its rivals,
\begin{equation}
    S(t)=\exp\big(-(t/\lambda)^{k}\big),
    \label{eq:weibull}
\end{equation}
where $k=1$ is the exponential law of Kramers escape, $k>1$ means a rising hazard, and $k<1$ means a falling hazard.

\para{Models and data.}
We choose one-hidden-layer tanh networks with cross entropy plus L2, because tanh gives exact Hessians, and because L2 and underparameterization give strict minima with nonzero per-sample gradients.
\Tabref{tab:landscapes} lists the three landscapes.
We take 12 minima on landscape A and 6 on landscape B, chosen evenly in $\lmax$ rank before any escape measurement and Newton-polished in float64 ($\|\nabla L\|<10^{-7}$, positive definite Hessian).

\para{Optimizers.}
We run SGD and Adam ($\beta$ values 0.9 and 0.999), with ablations for \rmsprop, \sgdm ($\beta=0.9$, $\eta=0.01$, same effective step as SGD at $\eta=0.1$), and warm-started Adam.
Each replica draws its own fixed-size minibatches.
We use $R=256$ at the base condition and 128 elsewhere (64 for default Adam), with a cap of 150,000 steps.
The simulation runs in float32 on shared GPUs (about 29 worker-hours); fits and Hessians use the CPU.

\para{{\bf What counts as an exit?}}
At geometrically spaced checks (10\% spacing, minimum gap 100 steps), we copy each replica and relax the copy by full-batch heavy-ball descent (\algref{alg:quench}).
A replica has exited if the relaxed point converges to a different minimum.
Times are interval-censored between checks.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Exit check for one replica}
\label{alg:quench}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} replica $\vtheta_t$, minimum $\tstar$, loss $L$, sharpness $\lmax$
\State $q \gets \max(1,\lmax)$; \quad $\tilde\vtheta \gets \vtheta_t$ \Comment{{\small\it work on a copy}}
\State Relax $\tilde\vtheta$ for $1{,}500\,q$ heavy-ball steps ($\text{lr}=1/\lmax$, $\beta=0.9$)
\If{$\|\tilde\vtheta-\tstar\|\le 0.3$} \textbf{return} in basin \Comment{{\small\it cheap gate}}
\EndIf
\State Continue relaxing up to $5{,}000\,q$ total steps
\State $d\gets\|\tilde\vtheta-\tstar\|$; \quad $b\gets$ barrier on the segment from $\tilde\vtheta$ to $\tstar$; \quad $\Delta L\gets L(\tilde\vtheta)-L(\tstar)$
\If{$d>0.05$ \textbf{and} ($b>10^{-5}$ \textbf{or} $|\Delta L|>2\times10^{-5}$)} \textbf{return} exited
\EndIf
\State \textbf{return} in basin \Comment{{\small\it slow soft mode, not a new minimum}}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This rule survived two failures, both found by testing it.
First, short quenches mislead: a 400-step quench reported 0--34\% exited where a converged quench reported up to 97\%.
Second, slow soft modes give false exits: a Newton audit found that 495 of the first 6,337 exits (7.8\%) polished back to $\tstar$, and in one minimum 71\% of exits were false.
We added the barrier-or-loss rule and repeated the affected runs with the same seeds.
A barrier test alone would be wrong, because one \digitsdata minimum has a real neighbour with no segment barrier.

\para{Experiments.}
E0 is a controlled 8-dimensional double well in the Gaussian minibatch-noise limit, with 1,000 replicas per condition.
It validates the instrument against Kramers, and it does not model a network.
E1 measures shape at all minima with SGD, $B=16$, $\eta\in\{0.1,0.2\}$.
E2 compares Adam with SGD at Adam learning rates $\{0.004,0.008,0.016\}$ on A and $\{0.001,0.002\}$ on B.
We interpolate the median in $\log\eta$ to the per-minimum matched rate, where matched means equal mean excess full loss over steps 500--2,000, well before most exits.
Matched rates are 0.0055--0.0109 on A and 0.00135--0.00152 on B.
We define wide and narrow as the bottom and top thirds by $\lmax$ ($n=4$ each on A).
E3 uses 6 (A) and 3 (B) minima with $B\in\{4,8,16,32,64\}$ at $\eta=0.1$, plus the equal-ratio triple $(\eta,B)\in\{(0.05,8),(0.1,16),(0.2,32)\}$.
E4 uses 30 unique saddles from mode-following Newton ($\|\nabla L\|<10^{-9}$), mostly index 2, with $R=128$ and $(\eta,B)\in\{(0.1,16),(0.02,16),(0.1,64)\}$.
The winning eigenvector has the largest absolute projection at first crossing of radius $r$ in the negative subspace.

\para{Baselines.}
For shape, the baseline is the exponential law, tested against Weibull, gamma, lognormal and shifted exponential.
For H3, the rival to a power law in $B$ is the Arrhenius form with $\log\tau$ linear in $B$.
For saddles, the baselines are chance (0.46 on average) and a linearized Monte-Carlo model with the measured per-sample noise covariance $\sigma_i$, in which component $i$ grows as
\begin{equation}
    z_i\,\sigma_i\,(1+\eta|\lambda_i|)^{t},\qquad z_i\sim\gN(0,1).
    \label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}

\para{Statistics.}
We fit all models by interval-censored maximum likelihood.
On simulated data, $k_{\text{true}}=0.7,1.0,1.3$ gives 0.71, 1.03, 1.29, with 5\% false rejection of $k=1$.
We apply Benjamini--Hochberg control at 5\% to likelihood-ratio tests.
Confidence intervals for $k$ use 100 bootstrap replicates of replicas, and cross-minimum intervals bootstrap {\em minima}, the unit of generalization.
When the median is not reached, we hold $\tau$ at the 150,000-step lower bound and flag it; Weibull extrapolations reached $10^{9}$.
Flagged medians are excluded from slope and factor fits.
H1 analyses use conditions with at least 50\% exits (28 of 36 E1 conditions).
All intervals are 95\%.
