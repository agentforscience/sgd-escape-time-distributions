\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\para{Kramers escape.}
\citet{kramers1940brownian} gives the overdamped rate over a barrier $\Delta E$ at temperature $T$ as $\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{a|b|}}{2\pi}\exp(-\Delta E/T)$, with $a$ and $b$ the curvatures at the well and the saddle.
At high barrier the exit is a Poisson process, so first-passage times are exponential ($k=1$).
Any deviation must come from outside that limit, such as a relaxation transient, a low barrier or a mixture of rates.
We use this exact result to validate our instrument, and then ask which of these sources act in real networks.

\para{SGD as an SDE.}
\citet{mandt2017stochastic}, \citet{smith2018bayesian} and \citet{jastrzebski2018three} derive a temperature that scales as $\etaB$.
\citet{zhu2019anisotropic} show that anisotropic noise aligned with sharp directions speeds escape from sharp minima.
With noise covariance close to the Hessian, \citet{xie2021diffusion} find mean escape time exponential in $(B/\eta)\,\Delta L/H$.
With noise proportional to the loss, \citet{mori2022powerlaw} find a rate that is a power law in the ratio $L(\vtheta_s)/L(\tstar)$, while $\log\tau$ stays linear in $B/\eta$.
\citet{ibayashi2021exponential} prove exponential escape efficiency without a stationary distribution, and \citet{simsekli2019tail} argue for heavy-tailed noise, whose L\'evy models give a polynomial dependence on barrier height.
No theory above predicts $\tau\propto\sqrt{B}$.
Unlike this line of work, which studies mean rates, we measure the distribution and its censored tail.

\para{Adam versus SGD.}
\citet{zhou2020towards} show with a L\'evy SDE that, for the same basin, SGD has a smaller escape time than Adam (\citet{kingma2015adam}), because adaptive scaling enlarges the basin's Radon measure and smooths noise tails.
\citet{xie2022adaptive} separate the two parts of Adam: adaptive learning rates escape saddles efficiently but do not select flat minima as SGD does, while momentum helps pass saddles and hardly affects flat-minima selection.
We found no direct measurement of an Adam-to-SGD escape ratio split by basin width.
Any such ratio depends on how the two learning rates are matched, so we make that calibration explicit and report how the result changes with it.

\para{Saddles.}
\citet{jin2017escape} and \citet{daneshmand2018escaping} bound the rate of saddle escape under perturbation or intrinsic gradient noise, and neither claims which negative eigendirection wins.
\citet{fukumizu2000local} and \citet{simsek2021geometry} show that unit duplication and symmetry create true saddles in small networks.
We add a measurement of the winning direction at nonsymmetric saddles found by mode-following Newton, and we compare it with a linearized model that uses the measured noise covariance.

\para{Sharpness caveats.}
\citet{keskar2017large} link sharpness to large-batch training, and \citet{dinh2017sharp} show that sharpness is not invariant to reparameterization.
We report $\lmax$ and trace in one fixed parameterization and use them descriptively.
\citet{yaida2019fluctuation} give fluctuation-dissipation checks for stationarity, which motivates our attention to the transient that follows a start at $\tstar$.
